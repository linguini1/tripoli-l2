# Scratch Ideas

## Motor

38mm motor is the final choice. It is easier to obtain in Canada and will also
be cheaper.

The motor needs a retention method. Based on what I've heard from others who
have done an L2, a retention method similar to an Aeropack retainer (like my L1)
is suitable.

## Recovery

Dual deploy, dual break. Drogue at apogee, main halfway down. Switchband coupler
to house deployment avionics, which will consist of between \[1, 2\] barometric
altimeters capable of deploying 2 deployment charges each (main and drogue) at
different altitudes. Altimeters will also need to be capable of deploying after
a set time-limit as an extra precaution.

Deployment will use black-powder charges detonated by e-matches.

## Airframe

Nosecone is PETG 3D-printed. Body tubes are fiberglass sock layup (6 layers,
light, 3in). Fins will be plywood through-wall so that I can have better thrust
transfer. Other materials are acceptable if I can acquire them.

[Fiberglass sock](https://sollercompositesllc.com/product/natural-uncolored-3-light-fiberglass-sleeves/)

I will have 2x 36" cardboard mandrels for the layup. The airframe body tubes
are:
* Forward: 14in
* Aft: 26in
* Switch-band exterior: 2in

In order to waste less sock, I will have one 18in mandrel for the forward +
switch-band layup. Then, I will use a 28in mandrel for the aft tube (inch of
slack on either end). Then there is an 8in coupler section for which I will
3D-print a 10in mandrel (to get the correct dimensions to be a coupler).

$18 + 28 + 10 = 56$ inches of total tube length. $58 \times 6$ for 6 layers gives
336 inches of sock required (28 ft). I will probably want to buy at least 30ft
(since that gives a discount on Soller anyways), but I may also want to account
for a complete screw-up on one of the tubes (let's say the coupler, since that
will be my test-tube). That would amount to 420in or 35 ft of stock.

## Avionics

\[1,2\] barometric altimeters for recovery deployment. Must perform on-board
altitude/charge detonation logging, be capable of firing two deployment charges
each and have some kind of audio indicator of arming and continuity check.

The avionics bay must be capable of supporting a redundant deployment
configuration.

The GPS tracker will go in the nosecone and at minimum must be capable of
providing coordinates of the rocket's landing location over long-range radio.
